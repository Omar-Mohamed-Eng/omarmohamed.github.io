Drug Safety Analysis: Adverse Effect Detection in an RCT

Hypothesis testing Classification Python

Objective — GlobalXYZ shared data from a completed randomized controlled trial. The question: are adverse reactions associated with the drug treatment at a statistically and clinically significant level, after controlling for demographic and physiological covariates?

Dataset & approach

Data from Hbiostat (Vanderbilt University Department of Biostatistics): sex, age, trial week, treatment group, white and red blood cell counts, and adverse effect occurrence/count per participant.

  1. Data quality checks — duplicate detection, missing-value analysis via Little's MCAR test, group-conditional imputation
  2. EDA — target balance, treatment vs. adverse effects, demographics, blood count distributions, effect rate over trial weeks
  3. Outlier analysis
  4. Statistical testing — Shapiro-Wilk normality checks, Welch's t-test / Mann-Whitney U, Chi-square with Cramér's V, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction for multiple comparisons
  5. Machine learning — ColumnTransformer pipeline, stratified k-fold cross-validation, Random Forest with balanced class weights, confusion matrix, ROC curve, feature importance

Findings

  • No statistically significant association between drug vs. placebo assignment and adverse effect occurrence, after BH correction.
  • No statistically significant association between sex and adverse effects.
  • A Random Forest baseline gives a multivariate view; its AUC-ROC reflects how hard the outcome is to discriminate given the available features.

Caveat — failing to reject H₀ does not confirm the drug is safe; it reflects the statistical power of this specific dataset.

YouTube Video Performance: Predicting Views from Pre-Publication Metadata

Regression Effect sizes Python

Objective — Given a draft video's pre-publication metadata, can cumulative-view performance be estimated, and which metadata fields actually carry predictive signal? Dataset: an API snapshot of 2,311 videos from the @freecodecamp channel (title, topic categories, publish date, duration, views, likes, comments).

Dataset & approach
  1. Business framing and success criteria; frozen-data loading and validation
  2. Missing-value and duplicate analysis; robust ISO-8601 duration parsing
  3. Leakage-aware feature engineering, focused EDA
  4. Statistical association tests with effect sizes and confidence intervals
  5. Mature-video retrospective modeling subset with a chronological train / validation / test split
  6. Median baseline, then from-scratch linear regression (gradient descent), Ridge regression, and HistGradientBoosting
  7. Test-set comparison, permutation importance, error analysis, limitations & next steps

These are observational findings, not causal effects — none of the associations below imply that changing a metadata field would change a video's performance.

Findings

  • View counts are extremely right-skewed (median ≈ 55.3K, max ≈ 49.1M) — modeling on a log target is far more stable.
  • Duration correlates with log views at Spearman ρ ≈ 0.568 (95% CI ≈ 0.540–0.596) — a substantial descriptive association, though views are also affected by video age and omitted factors.
  • Publication day shows a detectable distribution difference (Kruskal-Wallis H ≈ 179.1, p < 1e-35, ε² ≈ 0.075) — evidence of group differences, not that changing upload day changes performance.
  • On the chronological test set, Ridge was the best library model: MAE ≈ 1.22 on log views vs. ≈ 1.80 for the median baseline — about a 32% reduction in log-scale error.

Caveat — Ridge's test-set R² stayed negative, so pre-publication metadata alone doesn't explain enough variance to support a strong deployment claim — it beats the baseline on MAE but still leaves most of the variance unexplained. Raw-view MAE was actually worse than the baseline, a reminder that the choice of metric defines what "better" means.
